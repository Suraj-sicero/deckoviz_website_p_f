# 100 Ways to Fill Your Walls: Playlists and Collections for Your DASPort

## The Many Moods Of Your Home: For A Sanctuary That Actually Gets You

There's a version of ambient space-scaping and state design that's basically wallpaper with better resolution and vividity. And then there's the version where the home intelligence notices what mood the people in the space are in, what are they thinking about, what are they doing, the time, the occasion, the season, and it responds accordingly. The difference is the level of attunement and depth of understanding and breadth of generativity.

"What do I even put on this thing?" is the question almost everyone asks about a smart portal in the first week. This list helps answer the question. Your DASPort draws from three wells. The Deckoviz Global Library, full of fine art, photography, cinema, typography, and quotes styled to gallery standard. Your own personal library, your uploaded photos and media. And Vizzy's generative layer, which can create something new the moment you ask for it, such as an representational canvas in your palette, a poster in your words, a portrait study of a mood you can't quite name, or curate something old, so the wall stops being background and starts being a companion to your day

What follows is a hundred kinds of playlists and collections you can build, or ask Vizzy to build with you, each one a different lens on the same three wells. Some lean toward fine art. Some lean toward typographic posters, or aesthetic posters, or life posters. Some lean toward photography, global or personal. Most blend all three, because a good wall doesn't sit still in one lane. Think of this less as a menu and more as a map of the many things your DASPort can become.

A hundred is a lot of playlists. It's also not that many ways to live inside a year. Mornings and evenings, seasons and weather, the people you love and the moods you move through, a good wall should be able to hold all of it without repeating itself too often.

An art gallery shouldn’t feel like a slideshow. Random rotation gets old the way any unmanaged feed gets old, because a wall without intention is just another screen, and you already have enough of those. The DASPort is not trying to be a screen. It's trying to be a room, a place, a sanctuary, that thinks and creates with you, changes with your day, and says or expresses something deeply meaningful at exactly the right moment.

Once Vizzy has learnt enough about you, you don't need to build these by hand. Just tell Vizzy what you're after in plain language, "give me something for a rainy Tuesday afternoon" or "build me a gratitude playlist for Sunday mornings," and it'll assemble from here. But knowing the full range helps you ask better questions, and better questions are where the good home companions grow from.

### I. Portraits and People (1–7)

Portraits are where a smart portal stops being merely beautiful and starts being a mirror, literally and otherwise.

1. **Self-portraits.** For exploration, not vanity. Ask Vizzy for a self-portrait in the style of a Renaissance oil study, or a charcoal sketch, or a Hopper-esque solitary figure in a lit window, using your own uploaded photo as reference. Good for a personal study corner, or a "how do I see myself today" prompt on a birthday.
2. **Parent portraits.** A study of your mother in the style of a Vermeer domestic scene, your father rendered as a woodcut, a joint portrait aged into a matched sepia diptych. These make extraordinary gifts and even better everyday presences in a hallway, or to call upon for the times you miss your parents and they are just about to visit.
3. **Grandparent and elder portraits.** Slower, warmer treatments, oil or pastel, honoring rather than modernizing. A quiet fixture for a family room.
4. **Sibling and friendship portraits.** Playful, side-by-side compositions, pop-art duotones, or matching pencil studies. Good for a shared living space or a "us through the years" rotation.
5. **Couple and romantic portraits.** From your own photos, rendered as a single unified painting rather than two people cropped together. Works beautifully as the anchor piece for a bedroom or anniversary display.
6. **Children and milestone portraits.** First steps, first day of school, growth-spurt comparisons, styled consistently so the collection ages into a real archive rather than a scattered photo dump.
7. **General and abstract portraits.** Not everyone wants realism. Ask for a portrait rendered as brushwork abstraction, geometric fragmentation, or a single-line continuous drawing. Great for people who want the feeling of themselves on the wall rather than the likeness.

### II. The Twenty Modes (8–27)

Modes are less "collection" and more "operating state." Each one bundles art, posters, photography, pacing, and even suggested times of day into a single tap or voice command.

8. **Meditation Mode.** Slow-drifting mandalas, single-point-focus imagery, muted sunrise photography from the global library. Best at dawn or before sleep, five to twenty minutes at a stretch.
9. **Calm Mode.** Softer than Meditation Mode, more ambient than instructional. Watercolor washes, still-water photography, pale palettes. Good as a default background state for anxious afternoons.
10. **Romantic Mode.** Candlelit still lifes, intimate black-and-white photography, love poem posters, your own couple portraits woven in. Evening use, dimmed lighting recommended.
11. **Inspiration Mode.** Bold typographic quote posters, achievement photography (summits, finish lines, open roads), a brisker visual pace. Morning or pre-work use.
12. **Focus Mode.** Minimalist geometric art, muted single-tone canvases, almost no movement. Built for deep work blocks, not for browsing.
13. **Study Mode.** Lo-fi aesthetic visuals, rain-on-window loops, ambient library and reading-room photography. Pairs naturally with a study playlist running underneath.
14. **Work Mode.** Clean architectural photography, understated abstract art, nothing that competes for attention. A background hum rather than a foreground statement.
15. **Party and Get-Together Mode.** High-saturation pop art, confetti and light-trail photography, a fast rotation speed. Built for a living room with people in it.
16. **Creative Mode.** Deliberately eclectic, surreal juxtapositions, unexpected color pairings, art-movement mashups. Meant to jolt rather than soothe.
17. **Celebration Mode.** Fireworks, festival photography, milestone-themed posters (birthdays, graduations, anniversaries), warm gold tones throughout.
18. **Gratitude Mode.** Reflective quote posters, soft natural-light photography, journaling-adjacent prompts rendered as typographic art. Best paired with a Sunday morning or an end-of-day wind-down.
19. **Wellness Mode.** Spa and stretching imagery, botanical art, breath-paced slow transitions. Good for a home gym corner or a bathroom-adjacent frame.
20. **Travel Mode.** Global landmark photography and painterly cityscapes rotating through geographies you love or want to dream about, Kyoto to Marrakesh to Reykjavik in one sitting.
21. **Seasonal Mode.** Auto-adjusts the whole palette and subject matter to autumn, spring, monsoon, or winter, pulling from both global library and your own seasonal photos.
22. **Adventure Mode.** Playful, slightly game-like visuals, topographic art, expedition photography, illustrated maps. Good for a kids' room or a home office belonging to someone who'd rather be outside.
23. **Sacred Mode.** Spiritual and contemplative art across traditions, cathedral and temple photography, calligraphic scripture posters. Built for a dedicated prayer or altar corner.
24. **Family Mode.** Warm, joyful, deliberately busy with people. Personal photo albums, family portraits, togetherness-themed posters. The default for a kitchen or dining-adjacent frame.
25. **Dream Mode.** Surreal art, Dali-adjacent imagery, subconscious and imaginative visuals with no fixed narrative. Late-night use, low brightness.
26. **Morning Mode.** Sunrise photography, energizing color, affirmation posters in bright type. Set to trigger automatically at wake time.
27. **Evening Wind-Down Mode.** Deep ambers and blues, slow fades, reflective quotes. Set to trigger an hour before your usual bedtime.

### III. Wellness, Mind, and Daily Ritual (28–37)

28. **Meditation visuals collection.** Single-focal-point art, breathing-circle animations, still-lake and still-desert photography. Rotate slowly, twenty to fifty seconds per piece minimum, nothing should feel urgent.
29. **Visualization playlists.** Imagery matched to specific goals, a finished marathon, a built house, a mountain summit, curated to support manifestation practice or simple goal-priming before a big day.
30. **Workout playlists.** High-energy action photography, bold color-blocked posters, fast transitions synced loosely to a workout's pacing. Morning or pre-gym use.
31. **Affirmations collection.** Short, punchy typographic posters ("you are capable," "this too passes") set against clean backgrounds, cycling every ten to twenty seconds. Bathroom mirror-adjacent placement works well.
32. **Breathwork visuals.** Expanding and contracting circular forms, tidal photography, wave-rhythm loops timed to four-count breathing.
33. **Sleep-wind-down collection.** Near-static, near-dark imagery, deep blues and blacks, almost no motion. The goal is to disappear, not to entertain.
34. **Journaling companion collection.** Soft prompts rendered as posters ("what are you grateful for today"), paired with calm photography, meant to sit beside you while you write.
35. **Yoga and stretch visuals.** Botanical macro photography, sunrise-lit studio imagery, slow single-breath pacing.
36. **Therapy and reflection room collection.** Neutral, non-distracting art, muted tones, nothing narrative or emotionally loaded. Built to support a room where hard conversations happen.
37. **Spa and self-care collection.** Steam, water, stone, and candlelight photography from the global library, styled for a bathroom frame or a designated wind-down corner.

### IV. Quotes, Words, and Prayers (38–45)

38. **Inspirational quotes collection.** Bold, achievement-oriented lines from thinkers, athletes, and builders, rendered as clean modern typography. Morning-facing.
39. **General quotes playlist.** A wider net, wit, philosophy, literature, one-liners that make you stop for a second on your way past the wall.
40. **Poem posters collection.** Full short poems or striking single stanzas, set in serif type against textured paper backgrounds. Evening-facing, slower pace.
41. **Prayers collection.** Sacred texts and personal prayers rendered with restraint, calligraphic or minimal, built for a Sacred Mode corner.
42. **Mantras collection.** Short repeated phrases, often paired with Meditation Mode, designed for the kind of repetition that actually sinks in.
43. **Love notes and letters collection.** Handwritten-style typography, excerpts from real historical love letters (public domain sources) alongside space for your own words to Vizzy-generated posters.
44. **Wisdom-from-elsewhere collection.** Proverbs and sayings pulled from cultures and languages beyond your own, a small daily widening of perspective.
45. **Gratitude quotes collection.** Distinct from general quotes, deliberately narrow, every line oriented toward noticing what's already good.

### V. Time of Day (46–50)

46. **Morning-themed playlist.** Sunrise photography, energizing color, affirmation and inspiration posters. Best set to auto-trigger at wake time, five to ten minutes of gentle energy before the day gets loud.
47. **Afternoon-themed playlist.** Brighter, busier, more saturated. Cityscapes, activity photography, a good background for a working afternoon that still wants a little life on the wall.
48. **Evening-themed playlist.** Golden hour photography, warmer palettes, the transition piece between a productive day and a quiet night.
49. **Night and midnight collection.** Deep blues, starlight and astrophotography, near-still pacing. For the frame that's still on after everyone else has gone quiet.
50. **Weekend-morning collection.** Slower and softer than the weekday Morning Mode, unhurried photography, no urgency in the typography either.

---

### VI. Weather and Atmosphere (51–57)

51. **Rainy-themed playlist.** Window-glass reflections, umbrella photography, moody grays and blues, paired well with Study Mode or a slow afternoon.
52. **Foggy-themed cityscapes.** Skylines half-swallowed by mist, streetlights diffused into halos, a genuinely underused aesthetic that reads as more sophisticated than most "city" collections.
53. **Foggy landscapes.** Mountains and forests dissolving into white, minimal and painterly, excellent for Calm or Meditation Mode.
54. **Snow and winter-storm collection.** Fresh-snow photography, quiet white fields, occasional warm-lit-window contrast pieces.
55. **Sunny and clear-sky collection.** High-contrast blue-and-gold photography, best for Afternoon or Party Mode, an instant mood lift.
56. **Storm and drama collection.** Lightning, heavy cloud formations, ocean swells, for people who find weather thrilling rather than merely atmospheric.
57. **Golden hour collection.** The specific, beloved light of the hour before sunset, landscapes and portraits alike bathed in it, a reliable crowd-pleaser for any living room.

---

### VII. Seasons (58–62)

58. **Summer-themed playlist.** Beach and orchard photography, bright saturated art, both global library and your own summer photo uploads blended together.
59. **Winter-themed playlist.** Snowscapes, fireplace photography, muted warm interiors contrasted against cold exteriors.
60. **Autumn-themed playlist.** Turning leaves, harvest photography, amber and rust palettes, arguably the single most photogenic season in the global library.
61. **Spring-themed playlist.** Blossom photography, fresh green tones, a natural pairing with a Gratitude or Renewal-themed morning.
62. **Monsoon-themed playlist.** Heavy rain photography, wet-street reflections, particularly resonant for anyone with roots in a monsoon climate, personal photos slot in beautifully here.

### VIII. Places, Travel, and Cityscapes (63–72)

63. **City-themed playlist.** Skylines, street photography, and architectural studies from a specific city or a rotating world tour, your choice.
64. **Iconic landmarks collection.** The recognizable greats, rendered as fine art rather than tourist snapshots, a subtle but important distinction.
65. **Coastal and beach collection.** Cliffside and shoreline photography, calming blues, natural pairing with Wellness or Calm Mode.
66. **Mountain and alpine collection.** Summit photography, painterly ridgelines, good for Adventure or Inspiration Mode.
67. **Desert collection.** Vast, minimal, warm-toned landscape photography, an underrated choice for a Focus or Study room.
68. **Countryside and pastoral collection.** Rolling fields, farmhouses, slow-life imagery, a gentle counterweight to a mostly urban home.
69. **Architecture-focused collection.** Building studies pulled from across eras and cities, brutalist to baroque, framed as design reference as much as decor.
70. **Night cityscape collection.** Neon and streetlight photography, a livelier cousin of the foggy cityscape set, good for Party or Creative Mode.
71. **Hometown or personal-geography collection.** Built from your own travel photos and any place that matters to you specifically, the most personal entry in this whole section.
72. **World-tour rotation.** A single long-running playlist that slowly cycles through a different country or region every day, effectively a standing geography lesson on your wall.

### IX. Movies, Music, and Pop Culture (73–78)

73. **Movies and film-still playlist.** Iconic frames from cinema history, styled as art rather than screenshots, a great fit for a media room.
74. **Sci-fi movie posters collection.** Retro and modern sci-fi poster art, high visual drama, a strong pick for Party or Creative Mode.
75. **Classic film posters collection.** Golden-age and New Wave cinema poster art, elegant typography, good for a study or home office with cinematic taste.
76. **Album art and music-visual collection.** Iconic album covers and abstract visuals paired to whatever's playing, syncing your DASPort to your speakers.
77. **Directors and auteurs study.** A rotating collection built around a single filmmaker's visual language at a time, Wes Anderson symmetry one week, Tarkovsky stillness the next.
78. **Behind-the-frame collection.** Production stills and set photography from film history, a slightly more insider-facing companion to the poster collections.

### X. Occasions, Rituals, and Celebrations (79–87)

79. **Daily family dinner ritual playlist.** Warm, unhurried imagery timed to trigger automatically at dinner hour, designed to make the kitchen or dining room feel gathered rather than lit.
80. **Intimate time collection.** Soft, low-light, deliberately restrained art and photography for a bedroom, paired naturally with Romantic Mode.
81. **Date night collection.** A livelier cousin of the intimate collection, candlelight photography and warm typographic posters, good for the hour before guests or a partner arrives.
82. **Birthday and milestone collection.** Personal photos from the year gone by, paired with celebratory global-library imagery, a beautiful one-day-a-year showcase.
83. **Festival and holiday collection.** Culturally specific festival photography and art, rotating with the calendar, thanksgiving one month, Christmas the next, entirely configurable to what your household celebrates.
84. **Anniversary collection.** Built from your own relationship photo history, styled consistently year over year so the collection itself becomes a kind of growing archive.
85. **Bucket list posters collection.** Aspirational destination and achievement posters, part vision board, part travel wishlist, genuinely motivating when placed somewhere you pass daily.
86. **Housewarming and new-beginnings collection.** Fresh-start imagery, bright and optimistic, a good default for a newly moved-into space before you've built out your personal library yet.
87. **Graduation and achievement collection.** Personal milestone photos alongside inspiration-toned global art, built to mark a specific chapter closing.

### XI. Art and Culture Deep Collections (88–94)

88. **Art-movement rotation.** A slow tour through Impressionism, Cubism, Ukiyo-e, and beyond, one movement at a time, genuinely educational as ambient decor.
89. **Color-palette collections.** Curated purely by color rather than subject, a full wall of ochre one week, cool blues the next, a surprisingly powerful way to reset a room's mood.
90. **Black-and-white photography collection.** Timeless, high-contrast, works in almost any room and pairs with almost any other collection without clashing.
91. **Botanical and nature-study collection.** Fine-art plant illustration and macro photography, a quiet, grounding presence for a reading nook.
92. **Gardens and green-space collection.** The world's great gardens, photographed and painted, a favorite pairing with Calm or Wellness Mode.
93. **Libraries and reading-room collection.** Grand libraries from around the world, an atmospheric choice for a home office or study corner.
94. **Typography and design collection.** Pure lettering and graphic design as art, bold and modern, a strong fit for a creative studio space.

---

### XII. Personal Memory and Family Archive (95–100)

95. **Throwback collection.** Older personal photos resurfaced on a rotation, the DASPort equivalent of finding an old photo album in a drawer, except it finds you.
96. **Kids-growing-up collection.** A running, dated archive of your children's photos styled consistently over years, quietly becoming one of the most valuable things on your wall.
97. **Pet collection.** Because they deserve their own wall time too, styled as fine-art portraits rather than snapshots.
98. **Travel memory album.** Your own trip photography, curated and styled to match the destination's visual character rather than dumped in chronologically.
99. **"This day, past years" collection.** Vizzy surfaces personal photos from this date in previous years, a gentle, unscheduled kind of remembering.
100. **Legacy collection.** A long-horizon archive blending your oldest family photos with newer ones, built to be added to for decades, arguably the single most meaningful use of a DASPort there is.

### A Bonus Section With Some More

- **Wedding and vow collection.** Your own wedding photography styled as fine art rather than album scans, paired with vow excerpts rendered as quiet typography. A natural anniversary-week rotation, or a permanent fixture near the entryway.
- **Baby and nursery collection.** Soft-focus personal photography, gentle botanical illustration, pastel palettes with almost no visual noise. Built for a nursery frame that needs to soothe more than impress.
- **Science and space collection.** Deep-field astrophotography, orbital and planetary imagery, diagrammatic art of the solar system rendered with real elegance. A strong pairing with Focus Mode or a kid's room that's curious rather than cartoonish.
- **Ocean and underwater collection.** Coral, current, and deep-blue photography from the global library, slow-drifting and almost hypnotic. Pairs naturally with Calm or Meditation Mode, especially in a bathroom-adjacent frame.
- **Forest and woodland collection.** Canopy light, moss, and undergrowth photography, distinct from the fog and mountain sets in being close-up rather than vast. A grounding choice for a reading corner.
- **Food and culinary collection.** Still-life food photography and painterly kitchen scenes, from Dutch Golden Age fruit bowls to modern editorial food styling. An obvious, underused fit for a kitchen frame.
- **Sports moments collection.** Iconic athletic photography, the leap, the finish line, the exact instant of a win, styled as art rather than sports-page reportage. Strong Inspiration or Adventure Mode material.
- **Fashion and style collection.** Editorial photography and illustration spanning eras, from mid-century runway to contemporary street style. A confident, slightly daring choice for a dressing room or hallway.
- **Comedy and humor collection.** Witty one-liners, absurdist illustration, playfully mismatched juxtapositions. The one collection on this list that's allowed to just be funny, good for a kitchen or a kids' space that could use less solemnity.
- **New Year and resolutions collection.** Fresh-start typography, quiet horizon photography, goal-oriented posters timed to trigger every January. A yearly ritual piece rather than a permanent one.
- **Guest welcome and hospitality collection.** Warm, unfussy imagery timed to greet visitors, a soft "welcome" poster paired with photography of your home or city. Built for the entry hall or guest room, meant to make someone else feel considered.

### How to actually use this

You can handcraft these by yourself, or use Vizzy as your guide to design some or more. You can even say the name of the mode or collection to Vizzy, or just describe the feeling you're after, and it assembles the playlist live from the Global Library, your personal photos, or fresh generation, usually blending all three, once it has learnt enough. Ask it to adjust pace, palette, or brightness on the fly. You can combine two or more playlists: a Rainy-themed Study Mode, a Gratitude Mode built from your own family photos, a Sacred Mode timed to sunset. The hundred above are starting points, not boundaries.

The point of all of it is the same point a good home has always served: to reflect who you are back at you, at the right moment, without you having to ask twice.
